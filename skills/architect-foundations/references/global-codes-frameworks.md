# Global Building Codes and Regulatory Frameworks
## Comprehensive Reference for Architectural Design Compliance

> This reference covers the structure, scope, and key provisions of major building codes
> and standards worldwide. It is a navigational guide -- always verify against the current
> edition of any code cited.

## 1. IBC -- INTERNATIONAL BUILDING CODE (USA)

### 1.1 Scope and Structure

The IBC is a model code adopted (with amendments) by most US jurisdictions. Published by the International Code Council (ICC), updated on a 3-year cycle. Current edition: IBC 2021; IBC 2024 in adoption phase.

**35 Chapters organized as follows:**

| Chapter | Title                          | Design Impact                              |
|---------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| 1       | Scope and Administration       | Applicability, permits, inspections        |
| 2       | Definitions                    | Terminology used throughout code           |
| 3       | Use and Occupancy Classification| Determines A,B,E,F,H,I,M,R,S,U groups    |
| 4       | Special Detailed Requirements  | Atriums, high-rise, malls, underground     |
| 5       | General Building Heights and Areas| Type+occupancy = max height/area/stories |
| 6       | Types of Construction          | Type I-V based on fire resistance          |
| 7       | Fire and Smoke Protection Features| Barriers, walls, horizontal assemblies   |
| 8       | Interior Finishes              | Flame spread, smoke development limits     |
| 9       | Fire Protection and Life Safety Systems| Sprinklers, alarms, detection, suppression|
| 10      | Means of Egress                | Exit access, exits, exit discharge         |
| 11      | Accessibility                  | ADA/ABA compliance paths                   |
| 12      | Interior Environment           | Ventilation, lighting, space, sound        |
| 13-15   | Energy, Exterior, Roof Assemblies| Envelope performance, weather protection  |
| 16      | Structural Design              | Load combinations, general provisions      |
| 17      | Special Inspections            | Quality assurance requirements             |
| 18      | Soils and Foundations           | Geotechnical, foundation types             |

### 1.4 Key Design Interfaces

**Height and Area (Ch. 5):** Maximum building height (stories and feet) and allowable area per floor are determined by cross-referencing construction type against occupancy. Increases allowed for sprinklers (+1 story, +200% area per floor for sprinklered buildings) and frontage (up to 75% increase for open perimeter).

**Egress (Ch. 10):** Number of exits based on occupant load. Two exits required when occupant load > 10 (most groups). Three exits at > 500 occupants; four at > 1000. Exit separation: minimum half the diagonal in unsprinklered buildings; one-third in sprinklered.

**Accessibility (Ch. 11):** References ICC A117.1 and ADA/ABA. Accessible route from site arrival to all building levels and spaces. Accessible dwelling units: 2% Type A, 5% Type B minimum in R-2.

## 2. NFPA 101 -- LIFE SAFETY CODE (USA)

### 2.1 Scope

NFPA 101 addresses construction, protection, and occupancy features necessary to minimize danger to life from fire, smoke, fumes, and panic. It is not a building code per se but a life safety standard adopted independently by many jurisdictions (especially healthcare, existing buildings).

### 2.2 Structure

| Chapter Range | Content                                    |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------|
| 1-4           | Administration, definitions, referenced pubs|
| 5-7           | Performance/prescriptive criteria, egress   |
| 8-11          | Features of fire protection, building services, operating features, special structures |
| 12-13         | Assembly occupancies (new/existing)         |
| 14-15         | Educational occupancies                     |
| 16-17         | Day-care occupancies                        |
| 18-19         | Health care occupancies (most detailed)     |
| 20-21         | Ambulatory health care                      |
| 22-23         | Detention and correctional                  |
| 24-25         | One- and two-family dwellings              |
| 26-31         | Lodging, hotels, apartments, residences    |
| 32-33         | Residential board and care                  |
| 36-37         | Mercantile occupancies                      |
| 38-39         | Business occupancies                        |
| 40-42         | Industrial, storage, special structures     |

### 2.3 Key Provisions for Design

- **Egress:** Occupant load, number of means of egress, travel distance, dead-end corridors, common path of travel. Generally aligns with IBC but with specific differences (especially healthcare).
- **Healthcare specific:** Defend-in-place concept; smoke compartments max 2090 m2 (22,500 sf); corridor width 2440 mm (8 ft) min; suite provisions; hazardous area separation.
- **Existing buildings:** Separate chapters for each occupancy type address minimum requirements for existing buildings not undergoing major renovation.
- **High-rise provisions:** Buildings > 23 m (75 ft) require fire command center, voice/alarm, standby power, stair pressurization.
- **Atrium provisions:** Smoke control, draft curtains, fire sprinkler protection, travel distance through atrium.

## 3. ADA/ABA ACCESSIBILITY STANDARDS (USA)

### 3.1 Scope

The ADA Standards (2010 revision) apply to places of public accommodation, commercial facilities, and state/local government facilities. The ABA Standards apply to federally funded facilities. Both reference the same technical requirements.

### 3.2 Key Technical Chapters

| Chapter | Content                           | Critical Dimensions                     |
|---------|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| 3       | Building blocks                   | Clear floor space 760x1220mm; knee clearance; reach ranges |
| 4       | Accessible routes                 | Min 915mm wide; 1525 passing; 1525 turning |
| 5       | General site and building elements| Parking, stair, handrails               |
| 6       | Plumbing elements                 | Accessible toilet rooms, showers, bathing|
| 7       | Communication elements            | Signage, assistive listening, ATMs       |
| 8       | Special rooms                     | Assembly, judicial, detention            |
| 9       | Built-in elements                 | Benches, sales counters, food service    |
| 10      | Recreation                        | Play areas, swimming, sports, fitness    |

### 3.3 Critical Design Requirements

**Accessible route:** Continuous unobstructed path from site arrival to all spaces. Max running slope 1:20 (ramp: 1:12). Max cross slope 1:48. Min width 915 mm (1220 at U-turn around obstruction).

**Protruding objects:** Max 100 mm protrusion when 685-2032 mm above floor on wall; free-standing objects max 305 mm protrusion on post-mounted elements.

**Ground/floor surfaces:** Firm, stable, slip-resistant. Changes in level: up to 6 mm vertical, 6-13 mm beveled 1:2, over 13 mm requires ramp.

**Parking:** Accessible spaces per total: 1-25 total = 1 accessible; 26-50 = 2; 51-75 = 3; 76-100 = 4; 101-150 = 5; 151-200 = 6; 201-300 = 7; 301-400 = 8; 401-500 = 9; 501-1000 = 2% of total; 1001+ = 20 plus 1 per 100 over 1000. One in every six accessible spaces must be van-accessible.

**Assembly areas:** Wheelchair spaces: 4-25 seats = 1; 26-50 = 2; 51-150 = 4; 151-300 = 5; 301-500 = 6; 501+ = 6 plus 1 per 150 over 500. Companion seats adjacent. Sightlines over standing/seated spectators.

**Elevators:** Required when > 1 story (exceptions for 2-story with < 5000 sf per floor and certain occupancies). Car dimensions: min 2030 mm depth (for stretcher/gurney), 1730 mm width; standard min 1370 mm depth x 1730 mm width.

**Toilet rooms:** 1525 mm turning space; clearance at water closet 1525 wide x 1420-1525 deep; grab bars: side 1065 mm long at 840 mm; rear 915 mm long at 840 mm; lavatory knee clearance 685 mm; mirror max 1015 mm to bottom edge.

## 4. ASHRAE 90.1 -- ENERGY STANDARD FOR BUILDINGS

### 4.1 Scope

Applies to all buildings except low-rise residential (covered by IECC residential provisions). Establishes minimum energy efficiency requirements for building envelope, HVAC, service water heating, power, lighting, and other equipment.

### 4.2 Compliance Paths

| Path            | Description                                               |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Prescriptive    | Meet or exceed all individual component requirements      |
| Trade-off (envelope only) | COMcheck envelope trade-off; overall UA compliance |
| Performance (ECB method)  | Whole-building energy model must not exceed baseline|
| Performance (Appendix G)  | PRM method: proposed vs baseline energy cost         |

### 4.3 Key Provisions by Section

**Section 5 -- Building Envelope:**
- Maximum U-factors and SHGC by climate zone (1-8) for walls, roofs, floors, windows, doors.
- Continuous insulation requirements for steel-framed walls (effective R-values accounting for thermal bridging).
- Air barrier required: continuous across building envelope; max leakage 2.0 L/s/m2 at 75 Pa (tested) or prescriptive material/assembly approach.
- Vestibule requirements for climate zones 3-8 at building entrances.

**Section 6 -- HVAC:**
- Minimum equipment efficiencies (COP, EER, IEER, AFUE) for all HVAC equipment types.
- Economizer requirements by climate zone and system capacity.
- Energy recovery requirements based on outdoor air fraction and system size.
- VAV requirements for systems > 5 ton capacity in certain climate zones.
- Deadband between heating and cooling setpoints (minimum 5 degrees F).
- Simultaneous heating and cooling limitations.

**Section 7 -- Service Water Heating:**
- Equipment efficiency requirements.
- Pipe insulation requirements.
- Heat trap requirements for storage water heaters.

**Section 8 -- Power:**
- Voltage drop limits on feeders (2%) and branch circuits (3%).
- Automatic receptacle control (50% of receptacles in offices).

**Section 9 -- Lighting:**
- Lighting Power Density (LPD) by space type or whole building.
- Office (whole building): 7.5 W/m2 (0.70 W/ft2).
- Retail: 11.8 W/m2 (1.10 W/ft2).
- Mandatory controls: occupancy sensors, daylight responsive controls in daylit zones, exterior lighting controls.

**Section 10 -- Other Equipment:**
- Electric motor efficiency.
- Elevator efficiency provisions (2019+).

## 5. ASHRAE 62.1 -- VENTILATION FOR ACCEPTABLE INDOOR AIR QUALITY

### 5.1 Scope

Applies to all spaces intended for human occupancy except where other standards apply (e.g., ASHRAE 170 for healthcare, ASHRAE 62.2 for low-rise residential).

### 5.2 Compliance Procedures

| Procedure                | Description                                         |
|--------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Ventilation Rate Procedure (VRP)| Prescriptive OA rates per person + per area  |
| IAQ Procedure            | Performance-based; control specific contaminants    |
| Natural Ventilation Procedure| Operable openings meeting size/location criteria |

### 5.3 Ventilation Rate Procedure (Key Concept)

**Breathing zone outdoor airflow:**
Vbz = Rp x Pz + Ra x Az

Where: Rp = per-person rate (L/s/person), Pz = zone population, Ra = per-area rate (L/s/m2), Az = zone floor area.

**System-level adjustments:** Zone air distribution effectiveness (Ez = 0.5 to 1.0 depending on supply/return configuration); system ventilation efficiency (Ev) for multi-zone systems.

### 5.4 Natural Ventilation (Section 6.4)

- Operable openings: minimum 4% of net occupiable floor area.
- Location: within 8 m of operable opening (single-sided) or cross-ventilation path.
- Engineering analysis required if relying solely on natural ventilation.
- Mechanical backup or automatic window controls recommended.

## 6. IECC -- INTERNATIONAL ENERGY CONSERVATION CODE (USA)

### 6.1 Scope

The IECC establishes minimum energy efficiency requirements for new buildings and additions/alterations. Two sections: Residential (R-chapters for 1-3 story residential) and Commercial (C-chapters for all other buildings).

### 6.2 Climate Zones

| Zone | Description            | US Examples                              |
|------|------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| 1    | Very hot-humid         | Miami, Honolulu, Guam                    |
| 2    | Hot                    | Houston, Phoenix, Tampa                  |
| 3    | Warm                   | Atlanta, Las Vegas, Los Angeles          |
| 4    | Mixed                  | New York, Seattle, St. Louis             |
| 5    | Cool                   | Chicago, Boston, Denver                  |
| 6    | Cold                   | Minneapolis, Portland (ME), Burlington   |
| 7    | Very cold              | Duluth, Fargo, International Falls       |
| 8    | Subarctic              | Fairbanks, Barrow                        |

Sub-classifications: A (moist), B (dry), C (marine) based on precipitation/temperature patterns.

### 6.3 Key Residential Provisions (R-Chapter)

- Insulation R-values by climate zone for ceiling, wall, floor, basement, slab, crawlspace.
- Window U-factor and SHGC maxima by climate zone.
- Blower door testing: max 3.0 ACH50 (CZ 3-8), 5.0 ACH50 (CZ 1-2) in 2021 IECC.
- Duct leakage testing: max 4 CFM25/100 ft2 (total), 3 CFM25/100 ft2 (to outside).
- Lighting: 90% high-efficacy lamps or luminaires.

### 6.4 Key Commercial Provisions (C-Chapter)

- References ASHRAE 90.1 as equivalent compliance path.
- Envelope, HVAC, lighting, power requirements parallel to ASHRAE 90.1.
- Total building performance path available via energy modeling.

## 7. BUILDING REGULATIONS -- ENGLAND AND WALES

### 7.1 Structure

The Building Regulations 2010 (as amended) are statutory instruments. Technical guidance is provided by Approved Documents (ADs), which are not mandatory but offer a "deemed to satisfy" compliance route. Projects can demonstrate compliance through alternative means.

### 7.2 Approved Documents

| AD   | Title                                       | Key Design Impact                           |
|------|---------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| A    | Structure                                   | Structural stability, disproportionate collapse, wall thickness rules |
| B    | Fire Safety (Vol. 1: Dwellings; Vol. 2: Other)| Means of warning, escape, fire spread (internal/external), access for fire service |
| C    | Site Preparation and Resistance to Contaminants and Moisture | Substructure, damp-proofing, weather resistance |
| D    | Toxic Substances                            | Cavity wall insulation (urea formaldehyde)  |
| E    | Resistance to Sound                         | Airborne and impact sound insulation between dwellings and within buildings |
| F    | Ventilation                                 | Whole-dwelling ventilation, extract rates, purge ventilation, system types (1-4) |
| G    | Sanitation, Hot Water Safety, Water Efficiency| Water closet provision, hot water safety, 125 L/person/day optional target |
| H    | Drainage and Waste Disposal                 | Foul drainage, rainwater drainage, cesspools, wastewater treatment |
| J    | Combustion Appliances and Fuel Storage       | Air supply, discharge of products of combustion, hearths |
| K    | Protection from Falling, Collision and Impact| Stairs, ladders, ramps, guards, barriers, glazing safety |
| L    | Conservation of Fuel and Power (Vol 1: Dwellings; Vol 2: Other)| U-values, airtightness, HVAC efficiency, renewable energy, TFEE/BER compliance |
| M    | Access to and Use of Buildings (Vol 1: Dwellings; Vol 2: Other)| Accessible approach, entrance, circulation, facilities, sanitary provisions |
| O    | Overheating                                 | Mitigation strategy for residential overheating risk (from 2022) |
| P    | Electrical Safety (Dwellings)               | Notifiable electrical work, Part P compliance |
| Q    | Security (Dwellings)                        | Doors and windows: security standards       |
| R    | Physical Infrastructure for High-Speed Electronic Communications| In-building infrastructure, connection points |
| S    | Infrastructure for Charging Electric Vehicles| EV charging provision for new buildings     |
| 7    | Materials and Workmanship                   | Fitness of materials, CE/UKCA marking, workmanship standards |

### 7.3 Key Design Interfaces

**AD B (Fire):** Determines compartmentation strategy; fire-resisting construction requirements are based on building height, use, and compartment size. Residential buildings > 11 m require sprinklers. Buildings > 18 m: restricted external cladding, evacuation strategies, sprinklers mandatory. Post-Grenfell amendments: ban on combustible materials above 18 m for wall systems.

**AD L (Energy):** Sets Target Fabric Energy Efficiency (TFEE) and Building Emission Rate (BER) targets. 2021 revision introduced ~31% CO2 reduction for new dwellings and ~27% for non-domestic vs Part L 2013. Notional building method: proposed must beat notional specification. Future Homes Standard (2025): 75-80% reduction, no fossil fuel heating.

**AD M (Access):** M4(1) visitable dwellings (mandatory for all new); M4(2) accessible and adaptable (required by many local authorities through planning); M4(3) wheelchair user dwellings (3% requirement common in London planning).

**AD O (Overheating):** Simplified method using glazing limits and cross-ventilation, or dynamic thermal modelling (CIBSE TM59). Applies to new residential from June 2022.

## 8. EUROCODES (EN 1990-1999)

### 8.1 Structure

The Eurocodes are a suite of 10 European standards for structural design, published by CEN and adopted by EU/EEA member states with National Annexes providing nationally determined parameters.

### 8.2 Individual Eurocodes

| Code    | Title                                      | Key Content                                |
|---------|--------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| EN 1990 | Basis of Structural Design                | Limit states (ULS/SLS), load combinations, reliability classes, design working life |
| EN 1991 | Actions on Structures                     | Part 1-1: Self-weight/imposed; 1-2: Fire; 1-3: Snow; 1-4: Wind; 1-5: Thermal; 1-6: Construction; 1-7: Accidental; 2: Traffic; 3: Cranes; 4: Silos/tanks |
| EN 1992 | Design of Concrete Structures             | Part 1-1: General; 1-2: Fire; 2: Bridges; 3: Liquid-retaining; durability, detailing, minimum cover |
| EN 1993 | Design of Steel Structures                | Part 1-1: General; 1-2: Fire; 1-3: Cold-formed; 1-5: Plated; 1-8: Joints; 1-10: Toughness; plus bridge, tower, silo parts |
| EN 1994 | Design of Composite Steel and Concrete    | Part 1-1: General; 1-2: Fire; 2: Bridges. Composite beams, slabs, columns |
| EN 1995 | Design of Timber Structures               | Part 1-1: General; 1-2: Fire; 2: Bridges. CLT, glulam, connections, vibration |
| EN 1996 | Design of Masonry Structures              | Part 1-1: General; 1-2: Fire; 2: Selection/execution; 3: Simplified methods |
| EN 1997 | Geotechnical Design                       | Part 1: General rules; 2: Ground investigation. Design approaches 1/2/3 |
| EN 1998 | Design of Structures for Earthquake Resistance | Part 1: General/buildings; 2: Bridges; 3: Existing; 4: Silos/tanks; 5: Foundations; 6: Towers. Ductility classes, behavior factors |
| EN 1999 | Design of Aluminium Structures            | Part 1-1: General; 1-2: Fire; 1-3: Fatigue; 1-4: Cold-formed; 1-5: Shell |

### 8.3 Key Concepts for Architectural Design

**Design working life:** Category 1 (10 yr: temporary); Category 2 (10-25 yr: replaceable components); Category 3 (15-30 yr: agricultural); Category 4 (50 yr: buildings and common structures); Category 5 (100 yr: monumental, bridges).

**Consequence classes:** CC1 (low), CC2 (medium), CC3 (high) -- determines partial safety factors and reliability index targets.

**Load combinations:** Fundamental: 1.35 Gk + 1.5 Qk,1 + 1.5 SUM(psi0,i Qk,i). Accidental: 1.0 Gk + Ad + psi1 or psi2 Qk.

**National Annexes:** Each country provides nationally determined parameters (NDPs) that modify Eurocode values for local conditions (snow maps, wind maps, seismic zones, gamma factors, etc.).

## 9. NCC -- NATIONAL CONSTRUCTION CODE (AUSTRALIA)

### 9.1 Structure

The NCC is a performance-based code maintained by the Australian Building Codes Board (ABCB). Three volumes:

| Volume | Scope                                      |
|--------|---------------------------------------------|
| 1      | Commercial buildings (Class 2-9)             |
| 2      | Residential buildings (Class 1 and 10)       |

### 9.2 Building Classifications (Similar to IBC Occupancy)

| Class | Description                                          |
|-------|------------------------------------------------------|
| 1a    | Single dwelling (detached, row, terrace)             |
| 1b    | Small boarding house/hostel (< 12 persons, < 300m2)  |
| 2     | Apartments (sole-occupancy units)                     |
| 3     | Residential (hotels, motels, backpackers)             |
| 4     | Dwelling in a non-residential building                |
| 5     | Office                                                |
| 6     | Retail/shop                                           |
| 7a    | Car park                                              |
| 7b    | Storage/warehouse                                     |
| 8     | Laboratory/factory/workshop                           |
| 9a    | Health care                                           |
| 9b    | Assembly (school, church, etc.)                       |
| 9c    | Aged care                                             |
| 10a   | Non-habitable (shed, carport, private garage)         |
| 10b   | Fence, mast, retaining wall, swimming pool            |
| 10c   | Private bushfire shelter                              |

### 9.3 Performance-Based Framework

The NCC operates on a hierarchy: Objectives -> Functional Statements -> Performance Requirements -> either Deemed-to-Satisfy (DtS) provisions or Performance Solutions (alternative). Performance Solutions require assessment methods: evidence of suitability, verification methods, expert judgement, or comparison with DtS.

### 9.4 Section J: Energy Efficiency

Applies to Class 2-9 buildings. Key provisions:
- Building envelope (J1): thermal performance of walls, roofs, floors, glazing by climate zone (1-8, Australia-specific).
- Building sealing (J2): air leakage provisions.
- Air-conditioning and ventilation (J5): equipment efficiency, ductwork, controls.
- Artificial lighting (J6): LPD limits, controls, daylight.
- Heated water supply (J7): efficiency, insulation, solar/heat pump requirements.
- NatHERS rating for residential: minimum 6-7 stars (out of 10) by jurisdiction.

## 10. NBC -- NATIONAL BUILDING CODE OF CANADA

### 10.1 Structure

Published by the National Research Council of Canada (NRC), adopted and amended by provinces. Current: NBC 2020. Two divisions:

| Division | Content                                          |
|----------|--------------------------------------------------|
| A        | Compliance and general provisions                |
| B        | Acceptable solutions (prescriptive)              |
| C        | Administrative provisions (provincial adoption)  |

Part structure within Division B:
- Part 3: Fire protection, occupant safety, accessibility (major buildings)
- Part 4: Structural design
- Part 5: Environmental separation (envelope)
- Part 6: Heating, ventilating, air conditioning
- Part 7: Plumbing
- Part 8: Safety measures at construction/demolition
- Part 9: Housing and small buildings (prescriptive all-in-one for < 3 stories, < 600 m2)

### 10.2 Key Provisions

**Fire safety:** Occupancy classification (Group A: Assembly; B: Care/Detention; C: Residential; D: Business; E: Mercantile; F: Industrial/Storage). Noncombustible vs combustible construction; encapsulated mass timber (EMTC) provisions added in 2020 for up to 12 stories.

**Accessibility:** CSA B651 referenced; barrier-free design for all public buildings. Adaptable dwelling provisions increasing.

**Energy:** National Energy Code for Buildings (NECB 2020) is the companion energy code. Tiered approach: Tier 1 (baseline) through Tier 4 (net-zero ready). Provinces choosing adoption tier. Performance and prescriptive paths. Envelope, HVAC, lighting, service water heating, renewables.

**Seismic:** Based on probabilistic seismic hazard assessment. Site classes A-E (plus F for special). Seismic design categories. Force-based design with ductility-related force modification factors (Rd) and overstrength factors (Ro). NBCC seismic hazard maps updated periodically.

## 11. BUILDING STANDARDS LAW -- JAPAN

### 11.1 Structure

Japan's Building Standards Law (Kenchiku Kijun Ho, 1950, amended frequently) is administered by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT). Supported by the Building Standards Law Enforcement Order (BSL-EO) and various notifications.

### 11.2 Key Provisions

**Zoning:** 13 use districts (from Category I Exclusively Low-Rise Residential to Commercial and Industrial). Each zone specifies:
- Permitted building uses
- Building Coverage Ratio (BCR): 30-80% depending on zone
- Floor Area Ratio (FAR): 50-1300% depending on zone and road width
- Height limits: absolute limits in low-rise residential zones (10 m or 12 m)
- Shadow regulations (Nichiei Kisei): based on winter solstice shadow impact on neighbors

**Structure:** Seismic design is central. Two-tier approach:
- Level 1: Building must resist moderate earthquakes (C0 = 0.2) with no structural damage.
- Level 2: Building must resist major earthquakes (C0 = 1.0 for certain heights) without collapse.
- Route 1/2/3 for buildings under/over 31 m and over 60 m (performance-based for supertall).
- Base isolation and damping technologies widely used.

**Fire:** Fire-resistant construction required for buildings > 3 stories in fire prevention districts. Quasi-fire-resistant for 3-story wood frame. Fire compartments, evacuation routes, smoke control provisions.

**Setback regulations:** Front road setback (typically 1:1.25 or 1:1.5 slope line from opposite road boundary); north side setback (5-10 m + slope line to protect sunlight); absolute height districts.

**Building coverage and FAR bonuses:** Available for providing public open space (Kokai Kuchi), underground passages, or superior fire-resistant construction.

## 12. NATIONAL BUILDING CODE OF INDIA 2016 (NBC 2016)

### 12.1 Structure

Published by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) as SP 7:2016. Advisory in nature but adopted by many states and municipal corporations. 12 parts:

| Part | Title                                           |
|------|-------------------------------------------------|
| 0    | Integrated approach: prerequisites for building  |
| 1    | Definitions                                      |
| 2    | Administration                                   |
| 3    | Development control rules and general requirements|
| 4    | Fire and life safety                             |
| 5    | Building materials                               |
| 6    | Structural design                                |
| 7    | Constructional practice and safety               |
| 8    | Building services (plumbing, AC, electrical, lifts, etc.) |
| 9    | Plumbing services                                |
| 10   | Landscape development, signs, outdoor display     |
| 11   | Approach to sustainability                        |
| 12   | Asset and facility management                     |

### 12.2 Key Provisions

**Occupancy classification:** Group A: Residential; B: Educational; C: Institutional; D: Assembly; E: Business; F: Mercantile; G: Industrial; H: Storage; J: Hazardous.

**Development control (Part 3):** FAR by city category and plot size (1.0-3.5 typical); ground coverage ratios; setback requirements (front, rear, side); height limits related to road width and fire access.

**Fire safety (Part 4):** Detailed fire resistance ratings by occupancy and height. High-rise (>15 m): mandatory sprinklers, refuge areas, fire lifts, pressurized staircases, fire command center. Special occupancies (hospitals, assembly): specific provisions for evacuation.

**Structural (Part 6):** References IS codes: IS 456 (concrete), IS 800 (steel), IS 1893 (seismic), IS 875 (loads). Seismic zones II-V (India). Importance factors for different occupancies.

**Energy and sustainability (Part 11):** ECBC (Energy Conservation Building Code) referenced for commercial. Envelope, HVAC, lighting, electrical. GRIHA and IGBC rating systems for voluntary green building compliance.

**Accessibility:** Part 3 includes provisions for barrier-free access: ramps (1:12 max), accessible toilets, signage, parking (3% accessible), lifts in buildings > 15 m.
