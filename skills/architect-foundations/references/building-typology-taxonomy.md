# Building Typology Taxonomy: Comprehensive Reference

> Dimensions, metrics, adjacencies, and exemplars for every major building type.
> All dimensions in meters unless noted. FAR = Floor Area Ratio. NTG = Net-to-Gross ratio.
> Parking ratios in spaces per unit or per 1,000 sq ft GFA as noted.

---

## 1. RESIDENTIAL TYPOLOGIES

---

### 1.1 Single-Family Detached

**Plan depth:** 9-14m (deeper with central corridor or courtyard).
**Typical lot:** 300-800 m2 suburban; 150-300 m2 urban infill.
**FAR:** 0.3-0.6 suburban; 0.8-1.5 urban.
**Unit size range:** 90-350 m2 GFA (2-5 bedrooms).
**Stories:** 1-3.
**Parking ratio:** 1-3 spaces/unit (garage or driveway).
**Key metrics:** Lot coverage 30-60%; setbacks (front 5-8m, side 1.5-3m, rear 6-10m); private open space 40-200 m2.
**Critical adjacencies:** Entry foyer > living/kitchen; bedrooms clustered away from social zones; garage/utility accessible without crossing living spaces; garden/yard accessible from kitchen/living.
**Energy considerations:** Exposed on all four sides -- highest envelope-to-volume ratio of any typology; orientation critical for passive solar; roof area available for PV.
**Structural systems:** Timber frame (platform or balloon), light-gauge steel, masonry bearing wall, SIPs (Structural Insulated Panels), ICF (Insulated Concrete Forms); increasingly CLT and mass timber for premium market.
**Sustainability targets:** Envelope U-value 0.15-0.25 W/m2K (walls); air tightness 3-5 ACH@50Pa (standard) to <1.0 ACH@50Pa (Passivhaus); PV potential 4-10 kWp depending on roof area and orientation.
**Exemplars:** Farnsworth House (Mies, 1951 -- minimal single-room pavilion); Eames Case Study #8 (1949 -- kit-of-parts steel frame); Barragan House (1948 -- courtyard interiority); Maggie's Centres (various architects -- domestic scale for healthcare).

---

### 1.2 Townhouse / Rowhouse

**Plan depth:** 6-12m (party-wall constrained; 8-10m optimal for natural light penetration).
**Unit width:** 4.5-7.5m (narrow-lot: 4.5-5.5m; wide-lot: 6-7.5m).
**Stories:** 2-4 (plus basement and roof terrace in urban contexts).
**Unit size:** 80-220 m2 GFA.
**FAR:** 1.0-2.5.
**Parking ratio:** 0.5-1.5 spaces/unit (rear lane garage, integral ground-floor garage, or on-street).
**Unit mix:** Predominantly 2-4 bedroom family units; ground-floor units can include accessible/aging-in-place layouts.
**Key metrics:** Plan depth-to-width ratio ideally <2:1 for daylight; minimum 15% facade glazing on narrow frontage; stair width 900-1100mm; private rear garden 15-60 m2.
**Critical adjacencies:** Street entry at grade > stair hall > upper bedrooms; rear garden accessible from kitchen/dining at grade or half-level; party walls continuous for acoustic separation (STC 55+).
**Section strategies:** Split-level (skip-stop) for sloped sites; maisonette over flat; live-work with ground-floor commercial.
**Exemplars:** Georgian terraces, Bath and London (1720-1840 -- proportional facade, party-wall plan); Borneo Sporenburg (West 8 + various architects, Amsterdam, 2000 -- contemporary narrow-lot); Accordia (Feilden Clegg Bradley + Alison Brooks + Maccreanor Lavington, Cambridge, 2008).

---

### 1.3 Walk-Up Apartment (Low-Rise, No Elevator)

**Plan depth:** 10-14m (single-loaded corridor or dual-aspect units).
**Stories:** 3-5 (code-limited walk-up; 4 typical in European contexts, 5 in North American wood-frame).
**Unit mix:** Studio (30-40 m2), 1BR (45-65 m2), 2BR (65-90 m2), 3BR (85-120 m2).
**Units per core:** 2-4 (dual-aspect preferred; single-aspect max depth 8m).
**FAR:** 1.0-2.5.
**Parking ratio:** 0.3-1.0 spaces/unit (surface lot, tuck-under, or on-street).
**Core efficiency (NTG):** 80-88% (minimal corridor, no elevator, small stair).
**Key metrics:** Unit frontage 5-8m; balcony depth 1.5-2.0m; stair width 1.1-1.2m; maximum travel distance to exit stair 20-30m.
**Critical adjacencies:** Entry from street or courtyard > stair > unit entry; ground-floor units with private gardens or direct street access for accessibility; refuse/bicycle storage at grade.
**Exemplars:** Berlin IBA housing (1980s -- perimeter block with courtyard); Unite d'Habitation (Le Corbusier, 1952 -- skip-stop corridor, though with elevator); Nemausus (Jean Nouvel, Nimes, 1987 -- industrial aesthetic, double-height living).

---

### 1.4 Mid-Rise Apartment (6-12 Stories)

**Plan depth:** 12-18m (double-loaded corridor); 10-14m (single-loaded or point tower).
**Floor plate:** 600-1,500 m2 GFA per floor.
**Stories:** 6-12.
**Unit mix:** Studio 10-15%, 1BR 30-40%, 2BR 30-35%, 3BR 10-20%.
**Units per floor:** 6-16 (double-loaded corridor); 4-8 (point tower).
**FAR:** 2.5-6.0.
**Parking ratio:** 0.5-1.5 spaces/unit (structured podium or underground).
**Core efficiency (NTG):** 75-82% (elevator lobby, corridor, stair, mechanical).
**Key metrics:** Corridor width 1.5-1.8m; elevator: 1 per 80-120 units; floor-to-floor 2.9-3.2m; balcony 4-8 m2; minimum unit frontage 4.5m.
**Critical adjacencies:** Lobby > elevator/stair core > corridor > unit entry; amenity spaces (gym, lounge, co-work) at ground or top floor; parking below podium with secure access to lobby; refuse room per floor or chute system.
**Section strategies:** Skip-stop corridors (every 3rd floor) for efficiency; setback upper floors for terraces and contextual massing; podium base with tower above for density-with-streetwall.
**Exemplars:** 8 House (BIG, Copenhagen, 2010 -- figure-eight section, mixed-use); Barbican Estate (Chamberlin Powell & Bon, London, 1982 -- mid-rise urban fortress); Via 57 West (BIG, NYC, 2016 -- courtscraper hybrid).

---

### 1.5 High-Rise Residential Tower (13-40+ Stories)

**Plan depth:** 10-16m (point tower); 14-20m (slab tower with double-loaded corridor).
**Floor plate:** 500-1,200 m2 (point tower); 800-2,000 m2 (slab).
**Stories:** 13-40 (supertall residential: 40-100+).
**Unit mix:** Studio 5-15%, 1BR 25-35%, 2BR 30-40%, 3BR 15-25%, Penthouse 1-3%.
**FAR:** 5.0-15.0+.
**Parking ratio:** 0.3-1.0 spaces/unit (underground, often 2-3 below-grade levels).
**Core efficiency (NTG):** 70-78% (larger core for multiple elevators, pressurized stairs, mechanical shafts).
**Key metrics:** Core-to-perimeter distance max 12m for daylight; 2-3 elevators per 150-200 units; floor-to-floor 3.0-3.5m (3.5-4.5m for luxury); structural system: concrete core + flat plate (to ~40 stories), outrigger (40+), tube or diagrid (supertall); wind sway limit H/500 for comfort.
**Critical adjacencies:** Ground-floor lobby (double-height, 5-6m clear) > concierge/mail > elevator bank; sky lobbies at transfer floors; amenity floors (pool, gym, lounge) at podium top or upper floors for views; MEP floors every 20-25 stories; fire-refuge floors per code.
**Exemplars:** Aqua Tower (Studio Gang, Chicago, 2009 -- contoured floor plates); 432 Park Avenue (Viñoly, NYC, 2015 -- slender 1:15 ratio tower); Bosco Verticale (Boeri, Milan, 2014 -- vertical forest planting).

---

### 1.6 Co-Housing / Baugruppe

**Plan depth:** Varies; typically 10-14m.
**Stories:** 3-6.
**Unit mix:** Highly customized per group; typically 15-40 units with 30-40% common space (kitchen, laundry, workshop, guest rooms, children's play, garden).
**FAR:** 1.5-3.5.
**Parking ratio:** 0.3-0.8 spaces/unit (car-sharing common).
**Core efficiency (NTG):** 65-75% (higher common-area allocation reduces private NTG but increases overall functional efficiency).
**Key metrics:** Common house 80-200 m2; shared kitchen dining for 30-80 people; private units 50-120 m2 (smaller due to shared amenities); visual connection from units to communal courtyard.
**Critical adjacencies:** Common house adjacent to courtyard/garden; children's play visible from common kitchen; guest rooms near entry; workshop/maker space at grade with external access; shared laundry centralized.
**Exemplars:** Saettedammen (Denmark, 1972 -- first modern co-housing); R50 Baugruppe (ifau + Heide & von Beckerath, Berlin, 2013); Marmalade Lane (Mole Architects, Cambridge, 2019).

---

### 1.7 Student / Micro-Unit Housing

**Plan depth:** 10-16m (double-loaded corridor dominant).
**Stories:** 4-15.
**Unit size:** 12-25 m2 (micro-studio); 18-30 m2 (studio with kitchenette); cluster flats with 4-8 bedrooms sharing kitchen/living.
**Cluster flat size:** 80-160 m2 for 5-8 residents.
**FAR:** 2.0-6.0.
**Parking ratio:** 0.05-0.2 spaces/unit (bicycle parking: 1.0-1.5 per bed).
**Core efficiency (NTG):** 65-75% (high corridor and common-area percentage).
**Key metrics:** Bed density 200-400 beds/hectare; communal space 2-4 m2 per bed; laundry 1 washer per 20-30 residents; study space within unit or in common area; minimum unit width 2.7m; minimum ceiling height 2.5m.
**Critical adjacencies:** Entry/reception with parcel storage > elevator/stair core > residential floors; common rooms (lounge, study, kitchen) distributed every 2-3 floors; bicycle storage at grade; refuse centralized at grade.
**Exemplars:** Tietgenkollegiet (Lundgaard & Tranberg, Copenhagen, 2006 -- circular plan, communal kitchens); Carlaw Park Student Village (Warren and Mahoney, Auckland, 2014); The Collective Old Oak (PLP Architecture, London, 2016 -- co-living at scale).

---

### 1.8 Senior Living / Assisted Living

**Plan depth:** 12-16m (single-loaded corridor preferred for daylight and wayfinding).
**Stories:** 2-6 (low-rise preferred for evacuation; elevator-dependent above 2 stories).
**Unit size:** Independent living 45-80 m2 (1-2BR); assisted living 25-45 m2 (studio-1BR); memory care 20-35 m2 (studio).
**Unit mix:** Independent 40-60%, assisted 25-35%, memory care 10-20% (Continuing Care Retirement Community model).
**FAR:** 0.8-2.5.
**Parking ratio:** 0.3-0.5 spaces/unit (residents) + 0.1 spaces/unit (staff/visitors).
**Key metrics:** Corridor width 2.0-2.4m (handrails both sides); max travel distance to nurse station 30m (memory care), 60m (assisted); common dining 1.5-2.0 m2 per resident; outdoor garden 3-5 m2 per resident; nurse call in every unit and bathroom.
**Critical adjacencies:** Entry lobby with seating > reception/admin; dining room with servery adjacent to kitchen; activity rooms (crafts, exercise, chapel) near dining; clinic/therapy near assisted-living wing; memory-care unit with dedicated secure garden, looped corridor for wandering, and visual cue wayfinding; staff areas with sightlines to common spaces.
**Exemplars:** De Hogeweyk (Molenaar & Bol & van Dillen, Netherlands, 2009 -- dementia village); Humanitas Deventer (Netherlands -- intergenerational student-senior housing); Heald Farm Court (Pozzoni, UK, 2015 -- extra-care community).

## 2. COMMERCIAL TYPOLOGIES

---

### 2.1 Office -- Speculative / Multi-Tenant

**Floor plate depth:** 12-18m (core-to-window, each side; total 24-36m including central core).
**Floor plate size:** 1,000-2,500 m2 (boutique); 2,500-5,000 m2 (standard); 5,000+ m2 (trading floor / tech campus).
**Structural grid:** 7.5-10.5m x 7.5-10.5m (9m x 9m most common for flexibility).
**Floor-to-floor:** 3.6-4.2m (standard); 4.2-4.8m (premium with raised floor and high ceiling).
**Finished ceiling height:** 2.7-3.0m (standard); 3.0-3.6m (premium).
**NTG ratio:** 80-88% (low-rise); 72-82% (mid-rise); 65-75% (high-rise, larger core).
**FAR:** 4.0-15.0 (CBD); 1.0-3.0 (suburban campus).
**Parking ratio:** 1 per 30-50 m2 GFA (suburban); 1 per 80-200 m2 GFA (urban, transit-served).
**Key metrics:** Occupancy density 8-15 m2 NIA per person; elevator: 1 per 2,500-4,000 m2 GFA (quality of service: 25-30 second interval); MEP zone 1.0-1.5m floor-to-floor; raised floor 100-300mm; structural live load 3.0-5.0 kN/m2 (4.0 typical); column-free zone 9-18m from core to perimeter.
**Critical adjacencies:** Ground lobby (double-height, 100-200 m2) > turnstiles > elevator banks; retail at grade; loading/service separated from public entry; tenant floors: elevator lobby > open plan / demised suites; MEP risers grouped with restroom cores; roof: plant room, possible terrace.
**Exemplars:** Seagram Building (Mies, 1958 -- bronze curtain wall, plaza); HSBC Hong Kong (Foster, 1986 -- suspension structure, atrium); The Edge Amsterdam (PLP Architecture, 2015 -- BREEAM Outstanding, IoT-enabled workplace).

---

### 2.2 Office -- Owner-Occupied / Corporate HQ

**Floor plate depth:** 14-22m (often deeper for open-plan tech campuses; 14-16m for daylit cellular offices).
**Floor plate size:** 2,000-10,000 m2 (campus buildings can reach 20,000+ m2).
**Structural grid:** 9-12m x 9-12m (larger spans for open collaboration space).
**Floor-to-floor:** 4.0-5.0m (higher for exposed structure/services aesthetic).
**NTG ratio:** 70-80% (higher common-area allocation for amenities, town halls, labs).
**FAR:** 0.5-2.0 (campus); 4.0-12.0 (urban tower).
**Parking ratio:** 1 per 25-40 m2 GFA (suburban campus); lower in urban/transit contexts.
**Key metrics:** Amenity-to-office ratio 15-25% (cafeteria, gym, childcare, auditorium, maker lab); atrium volume for social interaction; floor plates supporting neighborhoods of 50-150 people; average walking distance to coffee/collaboration <60m.
**Critical adjacencies:** Arrival sequence (drop-off > lobby > reception/security > social stair/atrium); executive/boardroom suite with dedicated support; cafeteria/food service adjacent to outdoor terrace; auditorium near main entry with after-hours access; R&D/lab spaces with dedicated ventilation and loading.
**Exemplars:** Apple Park (Foster + Partners, 2017 -- 260,000 m2 ring, natural ventilation); Googleplex (NBBJ, various -- campus village model); Novo Nordisk HQ (Henning Larsen, 2014 -- undulating floor plates for collaboration).

---

### 2.3 Retail -- Shopping Center / Mall

**Floor plate depth:** 18-24m (anchor stores); 12-16m (in-line shops).
**Structural grid:** 8-10m x 8-10m (in-line); 10-16m x 10-16m (anchors, department stores).
**Floor-to-floor:** 4.5-6.0m (retail); 5.5-7.0m (anchor with mezzanine capability).
**Finished ceiling height:** 3.5-5.0m (in-line); 5.0-8.0m (anchor/food court).
**NTG ratio:** 55-70% (high circulation, common area, and service percentage).
**FAR:** 0.3-0.8 (suburban, single-level with parking); 1.5-4.0 (urban multi-level).
**Parking ratio:** 1 per 15-25 m2 GLA (suburban, peak-demand based); lower urban.
**Key metrics:** GLA (Gross Leasable Area) 10,000-150,000 m2; in-line shop width 5-8m, depth 12-16m; mall corridor width 4-8m (single-loaded) or 8-14m (double-loaded with kiosks); anchor stores 5,000-20,000 m2; food court 800-3,000 m2; service corridor 3-4m wide behind shops; loading docks 1 per 3,000-5,000 m2 GLA.
**Critical adjacencies:** Anchor stores at termini of mall axes (generating pedestrian flow between them); food court at central node or upper level; cinema/entertainment at upper level (destination driver); service/loading ring around perimeter, never crossing public circulation; vertical circulation (escalators + elevators) at 60-80m intervals.
**Exemplars:** Markthal Rotterdam (MVRDV, 2014 -- market + housing hybrid); Zorlu Center Istanbul (Emre Arolat + Tabanlıoglu, 2013 -- embedded in topography); Westfield London (Benoy, 2008 -- anchor-driven layout).

---

### 2.4 Retail -- High Street / Mixed-Use Ground Floor

**Unit depth:** 10-18m (from shopfront to rear wall).
**Unit width:** 4-8m (traditional high street); 8-15m (contemporary).
**Floor-to-floor:** 4.0-5.5m (ground floor); 3.2-3.6m (upper floors if mixed-use).
**Finished ceiling height:** 3.5-4.5m (ground retail).
**NTG ratio:** 85-92% (minimal common area for individual units).
**Key metrics:** Shopfront glazing: 70-90% transparency at ground level; threshold depth 0.5-1.5m (recessed entry); service access from rear lane or basement; live load 5.0-7.5 kN/m2; signage zone 0.6-1.2m above shopfront; column-free width minimum 4m.
**Critical adjacencies:** Sidewalk > shopfront > sales floor > back-of-house (storage, office, restroom) > service access/loading; upper-floor residential/office access separated from retail entry; refuse and recycling to rear.
**Exemplars:** Coal Drops Yard (Heatherwick, London, 2018 -- adaptive reuse, kissing roofs); Omotesando Hills (Ando, Tokyo, 2006 -- spiraling retail promenade); Via Montenapoleone Milan (historic luxury high street -- 5-7m wide shops, deep plans).

---

### 2.5 Office -- Co-Working / Flexible Workspace

**Floor plate depth:** 12-18m (similar to speculative office but with higher density zoning).
**Floor plate size:** 500-5,000 m2 per location.
**Structural grid:** 6-9m x 6-9m (tighter grid acceptable for smaller enclosed offices).
**Floor-to-floor:** 3.5-4.5m (exposed ceiling preferred).
**NTG ratio:** 60-72% (high proportion of shared amenity, event, and service space).
**Key metrics:** Density 4-8 m2 NIA per person (hot-desk); private offices 6-15 m2 (1-4 person); meeting rooms 1 per 15-25 members; phone booths 1 per 10-15 members; event space 50-200 m2; kitchen/cafe 1.5-2.5 m2 per 10 members; 30-40% of floor as collaboration/lounge; acoustic separation STC 40+ between offices.
**Critical adjacencies:** Reception/community manager at entry > lounge/cafe zone > open co-working desks > enclosed offices along perimeter or core > meeting rooms clustered near vertical circulation > phone booths distributed throughout > event space near entry with after-hours access.
**Exemplars:** Second Home Lisbon (Selgascano, 2016 -- biophilic open plan); WeWork prototype layouts; Factory Berlin (Julian Breinersdorfer, 2014 -- industrial conversion).

---

## 3. INSTITUTIONAL TYPOLOGIES

---

### 3.1 Primary / Secondary School (K-12)

**Classroom size:** 55-75 m2 (25-30 students, 2.0-2.5 m2/student).
**Floor-to-floor:** 3.5-4.2m.
**Stories:** 1-3 (primary); 2-4 (secondary).
**Site area:** 2-5 hectares (including playing fields).
**Key spatial requirements:** Classrooms with minimum 2.5m ceiling, 300+ lux daylight, cross-ventilation or mechanical; assembly hall 200-600 m2 (1.0-1.5 m2/student for full school); gymnasium 400-800 m2 (regulation basketball: 28x15m); library/media center 150-400 m2; cafeteria 1.0-1.5 m2 per sitting (2-3 sittings); admin suite 80-150 m2; science labs 75-90 m2 with fume hoods and gas; art rooms 75-90 m2 with north light and wet area.
**Critical adjacencies:** Admin/reception at main entry with visual control; early years clustered at ground level with direct garden access; science/art/technology in dedicated zone with shared prep; gymnasium near outdoor fields with external event access; cafeteria adjacent to kitchen and outdoor eating; library at heart of school as learning commons.
**Key metrics:** Overall area per student 8-12 m2 GFA (primary), 10-15 m2 GFA (secondary); corridor width 2.4-3.6m; toilet ratio 1:20 (primary), 1:25 (secondary); outdoor play 5-10 m2 per student.
**Exemplars:** Gando Primary School (Kere, 2001 -- ventilated roof, earth walls); Fuji Kindergarten (Tezuka Architects, 2007 -- oval roof as playground); Hellerup School (Arkitema, 2002 -- open-plan learning landscape).

---

### 3.2 University / Higher Education Building

**Lecture hall:** 1.0-1.5 m2 per seat (tiered); 150-500 seats; 4.5-8.0m floor-to-floor.
**Seminar room:** 2.0-2.5 m2 per seat (flat floor); 15-40 seats.
**Laboratory:** 8-14 m2 per researcher (wet lab); 6-10 m2 per researcher (dry/computational).
**Floor-to-floor:** 4.0-4.8m (teaching); 4.5-5.5m (research lab, for services and fume-hood exhaust).
**NTG ratio:** 55-70% (high circulation, specialist plant, and shared space).
**Key spatial requirements:** 300-500 seat lecture theatre with AV booth, accessible seating, acoustic treatment RT 0.6-0.8s; research labs with 1.5m bench module, fume hoods at 1 per 3-4 researchers, -12 Pa negative pressure; maker spaces with 5m clear height, 3-phase power, dust extraction; informal learning/social space 2-4 m2 per FTE student.
**Critical adjacencies:** Main entry/atrium as social condenser > lecture theatres near entry for shared scheduling; research labs clustered by discipline with shared equipment rooms; faculty offices adjacent to labs/seminar rooms; library/learning commons centrally located; cafeteria/commons at ground with outdoor terrace.
**Exemplars:** Rolex Learning Center (SANAA, 2010 -- continuous landscape floor); Saw Swee Hock Student Centre (O'Donnell + Tuomey, LSE, 2014 -- compact vertical stacking); Melbourne School of Design (JWA + NADAAA, 2014 -- timber diagrid atrium).

---

### 3.3 Hospital / Acute Care

**Department sizes (approximate):** Emergency 2,000-5,000 m2; Surgery suite 2,500-6,000 m2 (10-25 ORs); ICU 25-40 m2 per bed; inpatient nursing unit 35-50 m2 per bed (including support); imaging/radiology 1,500-4,000 m2; outpatient clinics 20-25 m2 per exam room.
**Floor-to-floor:** 4.5-5.5m (clinical floors, interstitial MEP preferred); 4.0-4.5m (admin/outpatient).
**Structural grid:** 8.4-9.6m x 8.4-9.6m (to accommodate OR 36-55 m2 clear, imaging suite lead-lined rooms).
**Stories:** 3-12 (horizontal campus preferred for wayfinding; vertical for constrained urban sites).
**Key spatial requirements:** Operating room 36-55 m2 clear (cardiac/neuro: 55-65 m2) with laminar flow ceiling, medical gas pendants, LED surgical lights; patient room 20-30 m2 (single-bed standard) with ensuite bathroom (5.5-7.5 m2), headwall with medical gas/data, family zone; nurse station with visual access to 8-12 beds; pharmacy near surgery and ED.
**Critical adjacencies:** ED at grade with ambulance bay, trauma rooms adjacent to CT scanner > surgery suite above/adjacent to ED with dedicated elevator > ICU adjacent to surgery; imaging central to inpatient and outpatient; inpatient nursing above diagnostic podium; outpatient clinics with separate entry and vertical circulation; kitchen/food service centralized with pneumatic tube or AGV distribution; loading dock for supplies, linen, waste (separated clean/dirty streams).
**Key metrics:** Overall 70-100 m2 GFA per bed; 2.5-3.5 parking spaces per bed; public corridor width 2.4-3.0m; patient corridor 2.4m minimum (bed-passing); elevator: 1 per 50-80 beds (bed-size elevators 1.4m x 2.4m car); HVAC: 15-25 ACH in OR, 6-12 ACH patient rooms.
**Exemplars:** Khoo Teck Puat Hospital (CPG, Singapore, 2010 -- biophilic healing garden); New Aalborg University Hospital (Friis & Moltke + C.F. Moller, 2020); Cedars-Sinai Advanced Health Sciences Pavilion (HOK + Foster, 2024).

---

### 3.4 Museum / Gallery

**Gallery module:** 6-12m clear span (minimum); 15-25m (large contemporary art); 4.5-7.0m ceiling height (permanent collection); 6.0-10.0m (temporary exhibition/installation).
**Floor-to-floor:** 5.5-8.0m (gallery floors, accounting for services above ceiling and raised floors).
**NTG ratio:** 45-60% (extremely high circulation, service, and climate-control space).
**Structural live load:** 5.0-10.0 kN/m2 (galleries); 15-20 kN/m2 (sculpture courts).
**Key spatial requirements:** Climate control: 21+/-1C, 50+/-5% RH (loan-standard, ASHRAE Class AA); lighting: 50 lux (works on paper), 150-200 lux (paintings), 300 lux (sculpture); UV <75 microwatts/lumen; art handling: freight elevator 3m x 4m x 3.5m clear; loading dock with air-lock chamber for climate-controlled truck unloading; crate storage 500-2,000 m2; conservation workshop 100-400 m2.
**Critical adjacencies:** Public entry > lobby/ticketing > orientation gallery > permanent collection enfilade or free-plan circuit; temporary exhibition with dedicated entry and flexible partitioning; education/lecture (150-300 seats) near entry; cafe/shop at entry with after-hours access; art receiving at grade, separate from public entry, with direct secure path to galleries and storage; conservation adjacent to storage.
**Key metrics:** Exhibition area 40-70% of GFA; circulation 15-25% of GFA; visitor density max 3-5 m2 per person; storage-to-exhibition ratio 2:1 to 4:1.
**Exemplars:** Kimbell Art Museum (Kahn, 1972 -- cycloid vault, natural light); MAXXI (Hadid, 2010 -- flowing circulation as gallery); Tate Modern (Herzog & de Meuron, 2000/2016 -- industrial conversion with Switch House extension).

---

### 3.5 Library / Learning Commons

**Reading room:** 2.5-3.5 m2 per reader seat; 300-1,000 lux task lighting.
**Stack area:** 6-8 volumes per m2 (open stacks); 15-20 volumes per m2 (compact mobile shelving).
**Floor-to-floor:** 4.0-4.8m (reading rooms, stacks with standard 2.3m shelving); 3.6-4.0m (admin/processing).
**Structural live load:** 7.5-12.0 kN/m2 (stack areas -- books are extremely heavy); 3.0-5.0 kN/m2 (reading areas).
**NTG ratio:** 65-75%.
**Key spatial requirements:** Quiet reading zones with acoustic separation (NC 30-35); collaborative zones (NC 40-45) with flexible furniture; digital/media lab with AV equipment; children's area (public libraries) with lower shelving (1.2m max), soft floor, acoustic buffer; special collections with climate control (18C, 45% RH); staff processing/cataloging 80-200 m2; auditorium/event space 100-300 seats near entry.
**Critical adjacencies:** Entry > self-checkout/info desk > browsing/new arrivals > reading rooms (gradient from social to silent); children's near entry with dedicated garden; special collections secure zone with researcher reading room; staff work areas behind-scenes with materials flow from receiving to processing to shelving; auditorium/meeting rooms accessible after hours.
**Key metrics:** 30-50 m2 GFA per 1,000 population served (public library standard); reader seats: 3-5 per 1,000 population; volumes: 2-3 per capita held.
**Exemplars:** Seattle Central Library (OMA/LMN, 2004 -- book spiral, program platforms); Oodi Helsinki (ALA Architects, 2018 -- civic living room); Exeter Library (Kahn, 1972 -- monumental concrete frame with interior light-court).

---

### 3.6 Performing Arts Center / Concert Hall

**Main auditorium:** Shoebox hall 1,500-2,500 seats (classical music, RT 1.8-2.2s); vineyard/surround 1,200-2,000 seats (RT 1.8-2.2s); proscenium theater 800-1,800 seats (drama RT 0.8-1.2s); opera house 1,400-2,200 seats (RT 1.3-1.7s).
**Stage dimensions:** Proscenium opening 12-16m wide x 8-12m high; stage depth 15-20m; wing space each side 8-12m; fly tower 2.5x proscenium height (25-35m total); orchestra pit 80-150 m2 (adjustable depth 2-3m).
**Floor-to-floor:** 6-12m (auditorium volume); 4.5-5.5m (foyer); 3.5-4.0m (back-of-house).
**NTG ratio:** 35-50% (extremely high support-to-performance ratio).
**Key spatial requirements:** Acoustic isolation from external noise (NC 15-20 for concert hall); room-within-room construction (isolated inner shell on neoprene pads); variable acoustics (retractable curtains, adjustable ceiling panels, reverberation chambers); 3-5 rehearsal rooms (1 full-size, 100-300 m2); 15-30 dressing rooms (10-15 m2 each); green room 40-80 m2; instrument storage 50-200 m2; scene shop 300-800 m2 with truck-height door; costume workshop 80-200 m2.
**Critical adjacencies:** Public: forecourt > foyer/box office > coat check > auditorium (multiple entry vomitories); foyer with bar/interval circulation (0.5 m2 per seat); performer: stage door > sign-in > dressing rooms > green room > stage (performers and public never cross paths); scene dock directly to stage at truck-bed height (1.2m); rehearsal rooms near stage with independent access.
**Key metrics:** Overall 3-8 m2 GFA per seat; foyer 0.5-0.8 m2 per seat; seat width 500-550mm; row spacing 850-1,000mm; maximum distance from stage to rear seat 30m (drama), 40m (concert), 45m (opera); sightline: every seat must see full stage (C-value 60-120mm).
**Exemplars:** Berlin Philharmonie (Scharoun, 1963 -- vineyard seating); Sydney Opera House (Utzon, 1973 -- sculptural shells); Elbphilharmonie (Herzog & de Meuron, 2017 -- concert hall atop warehouse, algorithmic acoustic panels).

### 4.1 Budget / Select-Service Hotel

**Rooms per floor:** 20-40.
**Floor plate depth:** 12-16m (double-loaded corridor).
**Corridor width:** 1.5-1.8m.
**Floor-to-floor:** 3.0-3.3m.
**Stories:** 3-10.
**Key ratios:** 0.3-0.5 staff per room; F&B area 0.3-0.5 m2 per room (limited to breakfast room); back-of-house 15-20% of GFA; parking 0.5-0.8 per room.
**Key metrics:** Room width 3.6-4.0m; bathroom pod 1.5x2.4m or 1.8x2.5m (prefabricated); structural grid matching 2 rooms (7.2-8.0m) x corridor module; 1 elevator per 100-150 rooms; housekeeping pantry 1 per floor (6-10 m2); laundry centralized or outsourced.

**Key metrics:** Room width 4.0-4.5m; bathroom 2.0x3.0m minimum (separate tub and shower at 4-star+); lobby 200-500 m2 (double-height, 5-7m clear); ballroom 500-1,500 m2 (column-free, 5.5-7.0m clear, subdivisible); fitness center 150-400 m2; pool 150-300 m2 (including deck); spa 200-600 m2; elevator: 1 per 75-100 rooms; main kitchen 0.5-0.8 m2 per F&B cover; linen storage 3-5 m2 per 100 rooms.

**Exemplars:** Aman Tokyo (Kerry Hill, 2014 -- minimal luxury); Park Hyatt Sydney (2020); Four Seasons Philadelphia (Foster + Partners, 2019 -- Comcast Tower integration).

### 4.3 Luxury / Resort Hotel

**Room size:** 40-65 m2 (standard); suites 70-250 m2; villas 120-500 m2.
**Rooms per floor:** 8-20 (low-density circulation).
**Floor plate depth:** 14-22m (allowing deeper rooms with views).
**Corridor width:** 1.8-2.4m (wider for luxury feel).
**Floor-to-floor:** 3.5-4.5m.
**Stories:** 1-6 (resort); 5-40 (urban luxury).
**Suite ratio:** 20-50% of room inventory.
**Key ratios:** 1.5-3.0 staff per room; F&B area 3.0-6.0 m2 per room (multiple restaurants, bars, room service, pool bar); back-of-house 30-40% of GFA; parking 0.8-1.5 per room (valet).
**Key metrics:** Room width 4.5-6.0m; bathroom 8-15 m2 (freestanding tub, rain shower, double vanity, water closet, bidet); balcony/terrace 6-20 m2; lobby 400-1,000 m2 (arrival experience is primary design moment); spa 500-2,000 m2; pool(s) 300-1,000 m2; landscape/garden area often 3-10x building footprint (resort).
**Critical adjacencies:** Arrival sequence choreographed for drama (drive through landscape, water feature, framed view); lobby as threshold between worlds; restaurants distributed across property with distinct identities; beach/pool/garden as primary program (resort); spa as destination with private treatment-to-relaxation sequence; service circulation fully concealed (back-of-house tunnels in resort settings).
**Exemplars:** Amanoi Vietnam (Kerry Hill, 2013 -- pavilion-in-landscape); Bulgari Resort Bali (Antonio Citterio, 2006); Morpheus Macau (Hadid, 2018 -- exoskeleton luxury tower).

### 4.4 Boutique / Lifestyle Hotel

**Room size:** 22-35 m2 (standard); suites 45-100 m2.
**Rooms:** 30-150 total (small-scale is defining characteristic).
**Floor-to-floor:** 3.3-4.0m (often constrained by adaptive reuse).
**Key ratios:** 0.6-1.0 staff per room; F&B area 2.0-4.0 m2 per room (destination restaurant/bar essential to brand); back-of-house 20-30% of GFA; parking 0.3-0.7 per room (urban, often valet or nearby garage).
**Key metrics:** Lobby as "living room" concept (60-200 m2, with bar, library, working areas blending); each room individually designed or themed; adaptive reuse of historic buildings common (warehouse, bank, mansion); restaurant/bar open to public (non-guests generate 30-60% of F&B revenue); rooftop bar/terrace as signature amenity.
**Critical adjacencies:** Street entry into lobby-bar (no separation -- public and guest mix); reception as desk or roaming (iPad check-in); restaurant independently accessible from street; rooms distributed across upper floors (irregular plans accepted for character); event space (if any) flexible and intimate.
**Exemplars:** Ace Hotel (various locations -- community-hub model); The Hoxton (Ennismore -- lobby as co-working); Hotel Il Palazzo (Aldo Rossi, Fukuoka, 1989 -- neo-rationalist boutique).

## 5. INDUSTRIAL TYPOLOGIES

---

### 5.1 Warehouse / Distribution Center

**Clear height:** 10-15m (e-commerce fulfillment); 8-12m (general warehouse); 30-45m (automated high-bay).
**Structural grid:** 12-25m x 12-25m (larger spans for racking flexibility).
**Floor slab:** 150-250mm reinforced concrete; flatness FM2 or better (VNA aisles); point load capacity 5-8 tonnes per post.
**Loading:** Dock-high doors (1.2m platform height) at 1 per 500-1,000 m2; truck court depth 35-45m.
**Key metrics:** Site coverage 40-60%; typical GFA 5,000-100,000+ m2; office/admin 3-8% of GFA; eaves height to underside of haunch; column-free preferred; fire sprinklers ESFR (Early Suppression Fast Response) for high-rack storage; floor load 50-80 kN/m2 (racking areas).
**Critical adjacencies:** Inbound docks > receiving/QC > storage racking > pick zones > packing > outbound docks (linear flow, no cross-traffic); office/admin overlooking operations; truck marshaling separate from car parking; hazmat storage isolated with bunding.
**Exemplars:** Amazon BHX4 (1 million sq ft fulfillment, robotic); Prologis Park (standardized speculative logistics); CJ Logistics Icheon (automated high-bay, 40m clear).

## 6. SPECIAL TYPOLOGIES

---

### 6.1 Religious / Sacred Space

**Nave/prayer hall:** 0.5-0.8 m2 per worshipper (seated pews); 0.8-1.2 m2 per worshipper (standing/kneeling with prostration space for mosque).
**Ceiling height:** 8-30m (scale creates transcendence; volume-to-plan ratio critical).
**Acoustic RT:** 1.5-3.0s (cathedral/liturgical); 0.8-1.2s (speech-focused worship).
**Key spatial requirements:** Narthex/vestibule as transition from profane to sacred; baptistery/ablution area; choir/organ loft; sacristy/vestry; bell tower/minaret; orientation (qibla for mosque, east for traditional church); natural light as theological instrument (clerestory, lantern, oculus).
**Critical adjacencies:** Approach sequence (street/garden > forecourt > narthex/vestibule > nave/prayer hall); altar/mihrab as focal terminus; side chapels or alcoves for private devotion; choir/organ in acoustic relationship to congregation; sacristy adjacent to altar with separate external entry for clergy; community hall/parish room with independent access for secular use; bell tower/minaret visible from distance as urban landmark.
**Key metrics:** Nave proportion: width-to-height ratio 1:1.5 to 1:3 (Gothic); 1:1 to 1:1.5 (Renaissance); seating capacity 200-2,000 (parish church), 2,000-10,000 (cathedral); mosque prayer hall: 1.0-1.2 m2 per worshipper including circulation; synagogue: 0.8-1.0 m2 per seat.
**Exemplars:** Ronchamp (Le Corbusier, 1955 -- light cannons in thick walls); Bagsvaerd Church (Utzon, 1976 -- cloud-form ceiling); Bruder Klaus Chapel (Zumthor, 2007 -- burned-core void); Church of the Light (Ando, 1989 -- cruciform aperture in concrete box).

### 6.2 Transportation Hub / Transit Station

**Concourse:** 1.5-2.5 m2 per peak-hour passenger (LOS C-D); ceiling height 6-15m for wayfinding legibility.
**Platform width:** 3-6m (island); 2-4m (side); platform edge doors increasingly standard.
**Key metrics:** Vertical circulation capacity: escalator 6,000-10,000 passengers/hour; stair 4,000-5,000 passengers/hour; elevator 300-600 passengers/hour; gate/fare array 25-40 passengers/minute per gate; maximum walking distance from entry to platform 300m (5-minute walk at 1.0 m/s); interchange distance between lines <200m.
**Critical adjacencies:** Street entry > ticketing/fare gates > concourse (clear sightlines to platform indicators) > vertical circulation > platform; retail along concourse edges (not obstructing flow); accessible routes paralleling every stair/escalator; kiss-and-ride and bus interchange at grade; taxi rank and bike parking at entries.
**Exemplars:** Oslo Opera House district integration (Snohetta, 2008); Crossrail/Elizabeth Line stations (various architects, London, 2022); Arnhem Centraal (UNStudio, 2015 -- continuous topological surface).

---

### 6.3 Sports / Arena

**Seating bowl:** 0.45-0.55 m2 per seat; seat width 450-500mm (standard), 500-600mm (premium); row depth 760-850mm; gangway every 20-40 seats.
**Concourse:** 0.8-1.5 m2 per spectator (concourse at vomitory level); ceiling height 3.5-5.0m.
**Playing field:** Football/soccer 105x68m; basketball 28.7x15.2m; Olympic pool 50x25m.
**Key metrics:** Ingress capacity: 660 persons per meter of gate width per hour; egress: full venue in 8 minutes (emergency); WC ratio 1:40 male, 1:20 female; F&B point-of-sale 1 per 150-250 spectators; VIP/suite 5-15% of capacity; parking 0.25-0.35 spaces per seat (suburban); maximum sightline distance 90m (football), 55m (basketball); C-value (sightline clearance) 90-120mm.
**Critical adjacencies:** Public: transport arrival > security screening > concourse (circumferential) > vomitories > seating bowl; VIP: separate entry > lounge > suite-level concourse > private boxes; media: press entry > press box > mixed zone > press conference room; athletes: team entry > locker rooms > warm-up > tunnel > field; loading for events: truck dock > floor-level marshaling > arena floor.
**Exemplars:** Allianz Arena (Herzog & de Meuron, 2005 -- ETFE pillow facade); Yoyogi Stadiums (Tange, 1964 -- suspended cable roof); Tottenham Hotspur Stadium (Populous, 2019 -- retractable pitch, dual-use).

---

### 6.4 Data Center

**Server hall:** 500-5,000 m2 per hall; clear height 4-5m; raised floor 600-1,200mm; cold aisle/hot aisle containment; power density 5-20 kW per rack.
**Structural grid:** 9-12m x 9-12m (accommodate standard rack rows at 1.2m spacing).
**Floor load:** 12-20 kN/m2 (loaded racks weigh 1,000-2,500 kg each on 0.6x1.0m footprint).
**Key metrics:** PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) target 1.1-1.4; N+1 or 2N redundancy for power and cooling; UPS runtime 10-15 minutes (bridge to generator); generator fuel 48-72 hours; network meet-me room for carrier cross-connects; fire suppression: gas (FM-200, Novec) not water; security: 5-7 layers from perimeter to rack.
**Critical adjacencies:** Electrical utility feed > transformer yard > main switchgear > UPS rooms > PDUs > server halls; cooling: chiller plant > CRAH/CRAC units > cold aisle supply > hot aisle return > heat rejection (cooling towers or dry coolers); generator yard adjacent to fuel storage with acoustic enclosures; loading dock for equipment (rack dimensions 600x1,070mm, but crates larger); NOC (Network Operations Center) with visual monitoring of all halls; office/support 5-10% of GFA.
**Exemplars:** Apple Data Center (Maiden, NC -- 100% renewable); Equinix IBX Data Centers (carrier-neutral colocation standard); Kolos Data Center (Ballangen, Norway -- fjord-cooled).

---

### 6.5 Convention / Exhibition Center

**Exhibition hall:** 5,000-100,000 m2 per venue; clear height 10-18m; floor load 50-100 kN/m2 (forklift access, heavy exhibit loads); column-free spans 30-80m+.
**Structural grid:** Minimum 18-30m x 18-30m (larger is better; 80m+ clear-span halls for major venues).
**Floor-to-floor:** 12-20m (exhibition halls); 4.5-6.0m (meeting rooms, prefunction).
**Key metrics:** Loading docks: 1 per 1,500-2,500 m2 of exhibit space; truck marshaling for 40-60 trucks simultaneously; meeting rooms: 20-100 rooms ranging 20-500 m2; ballroom: 2,000-5,000 m2 (column-free, subdivisible by airwalls into 3-6 sections); prefunction/registration: 30-40% of ballroom area; F&B concurrent service for 2,000-10,000 guests; utility floor boxes on 9-12m grid (power, data, water, compressed air for exhibitors).
**Critical adjacencies:** Public: transit/parking > registration hall > exhibition halls (linear or radial organization); meeting rooms above or adjacent to halls; ballroom with dedicated prefunction and catering kitchen; service: truck dock (at-grade) > marshaling > exhibit floor (level, no ramps); dedicated freight elevators for multi-level venues; 500-1,000 m2 event-organizer offices.
**Exemplars:** Fira Barcelona Gran Via (Ito, 2009 -- undulating roof canopy); ExCeL London (2000/2010 expansion -- linear dock-side layout); McCormick Place Chicago (1960-2007 -- largest US convention center, 250,000 m2).

### 6.6 Parking Structures

**Bay size:** 2.4-2.6m wide x 4.8-5.5m deep (standard); 2.7-3.0m x 5.5-6.0m (premium/accessible).
**Aisle width:** 6.0-7.3m (two-way, 90-degree parking); 3.6-5.5m (one-way, angled parking).
**Floor-to-floor:** 3.0-3.3m (flat plate); 3.3-3.6m (with services/sprinklers).
**Structural grid:** 5.0-5.4m x 15-16.5m (3 bays + aisle); long-span post-tensioned slabs preferred for column-free flexibility.
**Ramp grades:** Maximum 6.5% (helical); 12-15% (speed ramps, straight); transition grades 50% of main grade at top/bottom.
**Key metrics:** 25-35 m2 GFA per car space (including ramps, columns, walls); 300-500 cars per level (mid-size structure); turning radius 7.5-9.0m (inside edge); pedestrian stairs and elevators: 1 core per 250-400 spaces; ventilation: 6-10 ACH (naturally ventilated preferred via open facades); fire sprinklers throughout; EV charging: 20-50% of spaces (increasing requirement).
**Exemplars:** 1111 Lincoln Road (Herzog & de Meuron, 2010 -- open concrete frame with events); Caltrans District 7 HQ parking (Morphosis -- perforated metal screen); Santa Monica Civic Center Parking (Moore Ruble Yudell -- with rooftop garden).

---

### 6.7 Mixed-Use Podium-Tower

**Podium:** 2-6 stories; retail at grade (4.5-5.5m floor-to-floor); parking above (3.0-3.3m); amenity deck on podium roof.
**Tower:** Residential, office, or hotel above podium; setback from podium edge 3-6m for terrace/landscape.
**Key metrics:** Podium footprint typically 70-100% of site; tower footprint 25-50% of podium; podium FAR contributes 1.0-3.0; tower FAR contributes 3.0-12.0; structural transfer at podium-tower interface (transfer plate or transfer beams at podium roof level, typically 1.5-3.0m deep); separate vertical circulation for each use (retail, parking, residential, office); shared loading dock with use-specific service elevators.
**Critical adjacencies:** Street > retail frontage (continuous, active); residential lobby separate from retail entry; parking entry from secondary street or lane; residential elevator core from podium lobby through parking (secured) to tower; amenity deck (pool, gym, garden) at podium roof level between tower bases; MEP plant on podium roof or dedicated tower floors.
**Exemplars:** Marina Bay Sands (Safdie, 2010 -- three towers, sky park podium); Brickell City Centre (Arquitectonica, Miami, 2016 -- climate ribbon); Hudson Yards Platform (KPF/SOM/various, NYC, 2019 -- deck over rail yards).

## USING THIS REFERENCE

When developing a project brief or evaluating a design:

1. **Identify the primary typology** and its subtypes from this taxonomy.
2. **Check dimensional ranges** -- floor plate depth, structural grid, and floor-to-floor height constrain all downstream design decisions.
3. **Verify NTG ratio** against the project's efficiency target -- this is the single most important economic metric for developer-driven projects.
4. **Map critical adjacencies** as a bubble diagram before committing to plan geometry.
5. **Benchmark against exemplars** -- visit or study the referenced buildings for spatial quality, not just metrics.
6. **Cross-reference with local codes** -- dimensions here are industry standards; local fire, accessibility, and zoning codes may be more restrictive.
7. **Hybrid typologies** (mixed-use podium, live-work, co-living) should be analyzed as combinations of the base types, with particular attention to conflicting floor-to-floor heights, structural grids, and servicing requirements at transition zones.
8. **Future-proofing** -- design structural grids, floor-to-floor heights, and core locations to accommodate typological conversion (office to residential, retail to F&B, parking to habitable space) as markets shift over the building's 60-100 year lifespan.

## KEY METRIC DEFINITIONS

| Metric | Definition | Why It Matters |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| **FAR (Floor Area Ratio)** | Total GFA / Site Area | Primary density control; determines development yield |
| **NTG (Net-to-Gross)** | Net Lettable Area / GFA | Economic efficiency; 1% NTG = significant rental value |
| **Core-to-Perimeter** | Distance from elevator core to facade | Daylight penetration; max 12-15m for occupied space |
| **Floor-to-Floor** | Slab-to-slab vertical dimension | Constrains MEP distribution, ceiling height, structure |
| **Structural Grid** | Column spacing in both directions | Determines planning flexibility, span economy |
| **Plan Depth** | Building width perpendicular to facade | Controls daylight, ventilation, unit planning |
| **Live Load** | Design load for occupancy/use (kN/m2) | Structural sizing; varies dramatically by typology |
| **Parking Ratio** | Spaces per unit or per 1,000 m2 GFA | Major cost and area driver; declining in transit cities |
| **C-Value** | Sightline clearance above head in front | Auditorium/stadium design; 60-120mm standard |
| **RT (Reverberation Time)** | Time for sound to decay 60dB | Acoustic quality; varies by program type |
| **ACH (Air Changes/Hour)** | Volume of air replaced per hour | Ventilation adequacy; critical for hospitals, labs |
| **PUE** | Total facility energy / IT equipment energy | Data center efficiency; 1.0 is theoretical perfect |
