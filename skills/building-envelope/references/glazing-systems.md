# Glazing Systems — Comprehensive Reference

## 1. Glass Types

### 1.1 Float Glass (Annealed)

The base product from which all other glass types derive. Manufactured by floating molten glass on a bed of molten tin (Pilkington float process).

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Thickness range | 2–25 mm (standard: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 19 mm) |
| Density | 2,500 kg/m³ |
| Tensile strength | 40 MPa (edge strength governs design) |
| U-value (single pane, 6 mm) | 5.6 W/m²K |
| SHGC (clear, 6 mm) | 0.82 |
| VLT (clear, 6 mm) | 88% |
| Breakage pattern | Large sharp shards (dangerous) |
| Safety classification | NOT a safety glass per BS EN 12600 / ANSI Z97.1 |

**Body-tinted float glass:** Metal oxides added during manufacture. Grey (nickel), bronze (iron + selenium), green (iron). Reduces VLT and SHGC proportionally. Cannot selectively reject heat — tints reduce visible light along with solar gain.

| Tint | Thickness (mm) | VLT (%) | SHGC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grey | 6 | 43 | 0.59 |
| Bronze | 6 | 50 | 0.62 |
| Green | 6 | 75 | 0.60 |

### 1.2 Toughened (Tempered) Glass

Float glass heated to ~620°C then rapidly cooled (quenched), creating compressive stress on surfaces and tensile stress in core.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Strength | 4–5x annealed (120–200 MPa) |
| Thermal shock resistance | 200°C differential (vs. 40°C for annealed) |
| Breakage pattern | Small, blunt-edged granules |
| Safety classification | Class A / 1(B)1 per BS EN 12600 |
| Cannot be cut after tempering | True — must be cut to size before heat treatment |
| Spontaneous breakage risk | Nickel sulfide (NiS) inclusions cause delayed failure. Heat-soak testing (EN 14179) reduces risk to ~1 in 10,000 |

**Heat-strengthened glass:** Heated and cooled more slowly than toughened. 2x annealed strength. Breaks into larger pieces than toughened (not classified as safety glass alone). Used in laminated assemblies for better post-breakage performance.

### 1.3 Laminated Glass

Two or more glass plies bonded with an interlayer (PVB, SGP, EVA, or ionoplast).

| Interlayer | Thickness (mm) | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| PVB (polyvinyl butyral) | 0.38, 0.76, 1.52, 2.28 | Standard; good adhesion, UV block (99%), acoustic damping |
| SGP (SentryGlas Plus, by Trosifol/Kuraray) | 0.89, 1.52, 2.28 | 5x stiffer, 100x tear-resistant vs PVB; structural applications |
| EVA (ethylene-vinyl acetate) | 0.38, 0.76 | Decorative lamination; colored, fabric, metal mesh inserts |
| Acoustic PVB | 0.76, 1.52 | Enhanced acoustic damping (3–5 dB improvement over standard PVB) |

**Key properties:**
- Holds together on breakage (post-breakage safety)
- UV filtration: 99% UVA/UVB blocked by PVB
- Acoustic improvement: Rw +2–5 dB vs monolithic glass of same thickness
- Blast resistance: laminated glass is the basis of all blast-resistant glazing systems
- Fire: laminated glass does NOT provide fire resistance (PVB melts). Fire-rated laminated glass uses intumescent interlayers (e.g., Pilkington Pyrostop, Schott Pyran)

**Common configurations:**
- 6.4 mm (3+3 mm, 0.38 PVB): basic safety glass
- 6.8 mm (3+3 mm, 0.76 PVB): standard safety glass for balustrades
- 8.8 mm (4+4 mm, 0.76 PVB): enhanced safety, overhead glazing
- 13.52 mm (6+6 mm, 1.52 PVB): structural applications, balustrades, floors
- 17.52 mm (8+8 mm, 1.52 SGP): structural glass fins, canopies

### 1.4 Insulated Glass Units (IGUs)

Factory-sealed assemblies of 2+ glass panes with gas-filled cavities, separated by spacer bars and edge-sealed with primary (polyisobutylene/PIB) and secondary (polysulfide/silicone/polyurethane) sealants.

**Standard configurations:**

| Configuration | Description | Typical Thickness (mm) | U-value (W/m²K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4-16-4 | Double, air fill, no coating | 24 | 2.7 |
| 4-16-4 low-e | Double, argon, low-e on surface 3 | 24 | 1.1–1.3 |
| 4-16-4 solar control | Double, argon, solar control low-e | 24 | 1.1–1.3 |
| 6-16-6 | Double, air, no coating | 28 | 2.7 |
| 6-16-6 low-e | Double, argon, low-e | 28 | 1.1–1.3 |
| 4-12-4-12-4 | Triple, argon, 2x low-e | 36 | 0.5–0.7 |
| 4-16-4-16-4 | Triple, argon, 2x low-e | 44 | 0.5–0.6 |
| 4-10-4-10-4 | Triple, krypton, 2x low-e | 32 | 0.4–0.5 |

**Edge seal lifespan:** 20–35 years expected (IGU failure = fogging between panes). Warranty: typically 10 years.

**Structural IGU considerations:**
- Edge seal carries wind load shear across cavity
- Max panel size: typically 3.2 x 6.0 m (float glass) or 3.21 x 18 m (jumbo float, limited suppliers)
- Weight: 4-16-4 = ~20 kg/m²; 6-16-6 = ~30 kg/m²; triple 4-12-4-12-4 = ~30 kg/m²

## 2. Coatings — Detailed Performance Data

### 2.1 Low-Emissivity Coatings

**Surface positions (numbered from exterior):**
- Surface 1: outer face of outer pane
- Surface 2: inner face of outer pane
- Surface 3: outer face of inner pane (double IGU) — most common for low-e
- Surface 4: inner face of inner pane
- (For triple: surfaces 1–6)

**Coating position effects:**

| Position | Effect | Best Climate |
|---|---|---|
| Surface 2 (soft coat) | Reduces solar gain, reflects heat outward | Hot climates |
| Surface 3 (soft coat) | Reflects long-wave heat back into room, allows solar gain | Cold climates |
| Surface 2 + 5 (triple) | Solar control + heat retention | Temperate with overheating risk |
| Surface 3 + 5 (triple) | Maximum heat retention, allows solar gain | Cold climates |

### 3.2 Impact on Window U-value

The spacer bar creates a thermal bridge at the edge of the IGU. The effect is characterized by the linear thermal transmittance ψ_glass (W/mK), which depends on the spacer type and the frame type.

**Example — Double glazed, argon, low-e (Ug = 1.1 W/m²K), window 1200 x 1500 mm:**

| Spacer | ψ_glass (W/mK) | Uw (W/m²K) with aluminum frame | Uw (W/m²K) with timber frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aluminum | 0.08 | 1.9 | 1.5 |
| Stainless steel | 0.05 | 1.7 | 1.4 |
| TGI / warm edge | 0.035 | 1.6 | 1.3 |
| Swisspacer Ultimate | 0.028 | 1.5 | 1.3 |

For small windows, the edge effect is proportionally larger (more perimeter per unit area). Warm-edge spacers are most impactful on small windows and triple-glazed units.

## 4. Curtain Wall Systems

### 4.1 Stick System

**Description:** Mullions (vertical) and transoms (horizontal) are assembled on site from extruded aluminum profiles. Glass/panels are glazed in from the exterior using pressure plates and caps.

**Key dimensions:**
- Mullion width: 50–65 mm (face width visible from interior)
- Mullion depth: 100–175 mm (structural depth, determines span)
- Transom depth: 100–150 mm
- Typical module: 1200–1800 mm wide x 3000–4200 mm tall (floor-to-floor)
- Max span (mullion): 4200 mm without supplementary support (standard extrusion); 6000 mm with reinforced or steel-insert mullion

**Structural design:**
- Wind load: primary design criterion; mullion acts as continuous beam between floor fixings
- Deflection limit: L/175 or 19 mm (whichever is less) per AAMA CW-DG-1 / CWCT Standard
- Thermal movement: ±2 mm per 3 m at aluminum CTEs (23.1 × 10⁻⁶ /°C)
- Seismic: inter-storey drift accommodation ±25 mm (typical; per building drift limit h/400 to h/200)

**Installation sequence:**
1. Brackets fixed to floor slab edge (primary structure)
2. Mullions erected vertically, fixed top and bottom at each floor
3. Transoms fitted between mullions
4. Glass/panels installed from exterior (man-basket or scaffolding)
5. Pressure plates and caps fixed
6. Perimeter seal: fire stopping + acoustic seal at slab edge

**Advantages:** Lower cost than unitized; adaptable to irregular geometry; smaller crane requirement
**Disadvantages:** Slower installation (piece-by-piece); weather-dependent; quality dependent on site trades; higher risk of water infiltration

### 4.2 Unitized System

**Description:** Complete curtain wall panels manufactured in factory (glass, frame, gaskets, sealant all pre-assembled). Panels are typically 1.5 m wide x floor-to-floor height. Installed by hanging from floor slab brackets and interlocking panel-to-panel.

**Key dimensions:**
- Module width: 1200–1800 mm (standard); up to 3600 mm (oversized)
- Module height: floor-to-floor (typically 3300–4200 mm)
- Panel weight: 150–300 kg per module (double glazed)
- Stack joint: 25–30 mm between panels (accommodates floor deflection and thermal movement)
- Mullion joint: 20–25 mm between panels (interlocking male-female gaskets)

**Weatherproofing principle:** Pressure-equalized rainscreen (PER). The outer seal (rain barrier) deflects bulk water. The inner seal (air barrier) prevents air infiltration. The cavity between is drained and ventilated to equalize pressure, eliminating the driving force for water penetration.

**Performance standards:**
- Water penetration: zero at 600 Pa static pressure (AAMA 501.1 / EN 12155)
- Air permeability: <1.5 m³/h/m² at 50 Pa (CWCT Class AE) or ≤0.06 cfm/ft² at 1.57 psf (AAMA/NAFS A/W/PG Class)
- Structural: no permanent deformation at 1.5x design wind pressure; no glass breakage at 2.0x

**Installation rate:** 15–30 panels per day per crane (vs. 2–5 bays per day for stick system)

**Advantages:** Fast installation (3–5x faster than stick); factory quality; weather-independent quality; better air/water performance (factory-sealed gaskets)
**Disadvantages:** Higher cost (20–40% premium over stick); requires large crane; long lead time (12–20 weeks manufacturing); less adaptable to irregular geometry; larger transport logistics

### 4.3 Structural Silicone Glazing (SSG)

**Description:** Glass is bonded to the frame using structural silicone sealant, eliminating external pressure plates and caps. Results in a flush, frameless exterior appearance.

**Types:**
- 2-sided SSG: glass bonded on 2 sides (typically vertical mullions hidden), retained mechanically on other 2 sides
- 4-sided SSG: glass bonded on all 4 sides (fully flush exterior; most architectural but highest risk)

**Structural silicone requirements (ETAG 002 / EOTA):**
- Design strength: typically 0.014 N/mm² (short-term) to 0.007 N/mm² (long-term) per ETAG 002
- Bite depth (bond width): min 6 mm; typically 12–25 mm calculated from wind load
- Compatibility: silicone must be tested with specific glass coating, spacer sealant, and backing materials
- Factory application mandatory for warranty (most manufacturers will not warrant site-applied structural silicone)
- UV resistance: structural silicone must be UV-stable (black silicone preferred for longevity)

**Products:** Dow Corning 995 / 983, Sika SG-500, Tremco Spectrem, Momentive ISM

### 4.4 Cable-Net Facade

**Description:** A pre-tensioned network of stainless steel cables (horizontal and vertical) supporting point-fixed glass panels. Creates maximum transparency with minimal structure.

**Key properties:**
- Cable diameter: 10–36 mm stainless steel spiral strand
- Pre-tension: 20–100 kN per cable (depending on span and load)
- Grid spacing: 1200–2000 mm
- Max span: 20–30 m (height); cable deflection governs design
- Glass fixing: point-fixed via articulated stainless steel fittings (spider clamps or bolted)
- Deflection: L/50 typical (cable-net facades are inherently flexible)
- Glass type: toughened or heat-strengthened laminated (must accommodate movement)

**Examples:** Apple Fifth Avenue (New York), Berlin Hauptbahnhof, Kempinski Hotel Munich

**Advantages:** Maximum transparency, architectural drama, lightweight structure
**Disadvantages:** Very expensive ($1,500–3,000/m²), specialist engineering, movement sensitive, limited thermal performance (typically single-glazed or double with thermal concerns at fittings)

### 4.5 Point-Fixed Glazing (Bolted)

**Description:** Glass panels fixed to structure via point-fixings (bolts through countersunk holes in the glass) to steel/stainless steel support arms (spiders, fin supports).

**Fixing types:**
- Countersunk bolt: flush exterior; hole drilled in glass with bush/gasket isolator
- Button fixing: smaller hole, faster installation, lower load capacity
- Clamp fixing: no hole in glass; clamped at corners/edges (less elegant but more forgiving)

**Glass requirements:**
- Min 12 mm toughened (fully tempered); 15 mm for larger panels
- Heat-soak tested to reduce NiS failure risk (EN 14179-1)
- Hole diameter: 26–42 mm depending on bolt size; min 2.5x glass thickness from edge, 2x glass thickness from corner
- Laminated toughened for overhead and safety applications

**Support structures:** Stainless steel arms (spiders), glass fins, cable trusses, steel trusses

### 6.2 Thermal Break Design in Aluminum Frames

Standard aluminum has thermal conductivity of 160 W/mK — 4000x that of insulation. Without a thermal break, aluminum frames create extreme thermal bridges.

**Thermal break types:**
- **Pour-and-debridge:** Polyurethane poured into channel between inner and outer aluminum sections, then bridging aluminum is cut away. Oldest method; limited break depth (15–25 mm). Uf ~2.5–4.0 W/m²K.
- **Polyamide strip:** Pre-formed glass-fiber reinforced polyamide (PA66-GF25) strips crimped or rolled into grooves in inner and outer sections. Break depth 24–50 mm. Uf ~1.5–2.5 W/m²K. Industry standard. Manufacturers: Ensinger (Insulbar), Technoform (hi).
- **Deep thermal break:** Extended polyamide strips, 50–76 mm deep. Enables Uf <1.5 W/m²K. Used for Passive House aluminum windows (e.g., Schuco FW 50+ SI, Reynaers MasterLine 8).

**Passive House certified aluminum frames:**
- Schuco FW 50+ SI: Uf = 0.97 W/m²K (curtain wall)
- Reynaers CW 50-SC HI: Uf = 1.0 W/m²K
- Wicona WICLINE 75 evo: Uf = 0.93 W/m²K (window)
- These require 60–76 mm thermal break depth with foam-filled insulating zones

## 7. Installation Details for Common Window Types

### 7.1 Punched Window in Masonry Cavity Wall

**Position:** Window installed at the insulation line (typically 1/3 from exterior face). The frame overlaps the cavity insulation to minimize the thermal bridge at the jamb/head/sill.

**Key details:**
- Cavity closer: insulated cavity closer (min 50 mm insulation) at window jamb, head, and sill
- DPC tray: above window head, extending 150 mm beyond opening each side, with stop ends and weep holes at 450 mm centers
- Sill: projecting 30 mm min beyond wall face, with drip groove underneath, sloped 15° outward
- Internal plaster return: insulated reveal (30–50 mm insulation) to reduce thermal bridge
- Air seal: continuous air barrier sealed to window frame with tape (interior VCL lapped to frame)
- Fire stopping: mineral wool packing in cavity at jamb and head (no PU foam in cavity for buildings >18 m)
- Fixing: frame screws into masonry reveal at 150 mm from corners, then 600 mm max centers

**Thermal bridge at jamb:** ψ = 0.04–0.08 W/mK (with insulated reveal); 0.10–0.15 without reveal insulation.

### 7.2 Window in Timber Frame Wall

**Position:** Window fitted to structural opening in OSB sheathing. Frame screwed to timber studs/header/cill. Insulation wraps continuously around frame on exterior side.

**Key details:**
- Sheathing tape: air barrier (OSB) sealed to window frame with proprietary tape (e.g., SIGA Fentrim IS 20, Pro Clima Tescon Profil)
- Internal VCL: smart VCL lapped and sealed to window frame with tape (airtight layer continuity)
- External insulation: continuous over studs and around window frame (external insulation wraps into reveal)
- Sill membrane: waterproof membrane under sill, turned up 100 mm at sides, draining outward
- Drip flashing: above window head, integrated with breather membrane
- Window support: structural timber cill, shimmed and packed, insulated beneath

**Thermal bridge (Passive House install):** ψ ≤ 0.01 W/mK achievable with:
- Window positioned in insulation plane
- Insulation return over frame (30 mm min)
- Insulated sub-frame (Purenit / Compacfoam block around opening)

### 7.3 Window in Curtain Wall System

**Position:** Glass is an integral part of the curtain wall system — not a separate window in a wall.

**Vision panel (operable):**
- Concealed within curtain wall module
- Types: parallel opening (top-hung, projects outward 100–150 mm), side-hung casement (limited to small sizes), bottom-hung hopper (inward opening)
- Max size for natural ventilation: typically 600 x 1200 mm operable panel within a 1500 x 3500 mm curtain wall module
- Acoustic: operable panels reduce facade acoustic performance by 5–10 dB when open
- Weather rating: must achieve same air/water/structural performance as fixed panels when closed

**Spandrel zone (at floor slab):**
- Opaque panel concealing slab edge, services, and floor construction
- Build-up: shadow box (glass + air gap + insulated backing panel) or opaque insulated spandrel panel
- Fire requirement: E30/EI30 fire-rated backing (mineral wool + steel sheet) per floor-to-floor compartmentation
- Insulation: 100 mm mineral wool min behind spandrel (U-value ≤0.30 W/m²K for spandrel zone)

### 7.4 Roof Light / Skylight

**Types:**
- Flat rooflight: frameless glass on upstand (Velux, Glazing Vision). Double or triple glazed
- Dome rooflight: polycarbonate or acrylic dome on kerb. Low cost but poor U-value
- Barrel vault: continuous curved glazing over atrium. Aluminum or steel frame
- Pyramid rooflight: four-sided pyramid on square upstand

**Key details:**
- Upstand height: min 150 mm above finished roof level (min 300 mm recommended for green roofs)
- Insulation: continuous from roof insulation up and over upstand kerb
- Drainage: falls away from rooflight on all sides; overflow provisions
- Solar control: external blinds or high-SHGC rejection glass (rooflights receive 2–3x the solar gain of vertical glazing)
- U-value: same or better than vertical windows per code (UK Part L: 1.2 W/m²K max; Passive House: 0.80 W/m²K)
- Condensation: high risk — warm moist air rises. Triple glazing + warm-edge spacer + ventilation essential
- Safety: must be laminated to underside (overhead glazing). Walk-on rooflights: toughened + laminated, min 30 mm total, load-tested

### 7.5 Structural Glazing Interface

**Glass fin support:**
- Vertical glass fins act as structural mullions, supporting facade glazing against wind load
- Fin thickness: 12–25 mm toughened + heat-strengthened laminated
- Fin depth: 150–400 mm (span/15 to span/10)
- Connection: point-fixed via stainless steel brackets at top, bottom, and intermediate supports
- Deflection: L/175 or 19 mm
- Maximum fin span: 6–12 m (depending on depth and wind load)

**Bolted point fixing detail:**
- Countersunk bolt through glass into articulated fitting
- Bush: nylon or EPDM isolating bush between bolt and glass hole (prevents glass-to-metal contact)
- Rotation: fitting allows ±3° rotation in all axes to accommodate thermal movement and building deflection
- Typical bolt: M16 or M20 stainless steel 316
- Hole diameter: 26 mm (M16) or 32 mm (M20) with 42 mm countersink on exterior face
- Edge distance: min 2.5x glass thickness from edge; min 100 mm from corner
